# Add the bounded Sector Sprint table

Sector Sprint is the fifth Grand Salon table: an original, code-drawn side-scrolling Chandigarh action comedy for adults, led by Gurpreet and his mother Harjit. It combines automatic forward motion with optional variable-height leaps, one air step, dash, contextual vault, aerial stomp, five Act-local harmless tools, three deterministic temporary effects, escalating authored scenes, Punjabi-family wit, and a five-Act city journey while rejecting an endless route, score, high score, health, lives, failure loop, randomized loot, persistent inventory, and fatigue-to-sleep framing.

The action route has five fixed Acts: Ghar Wapsi, Sabzi Command, Baraat Detour, Monsoon Protocol, and Roti Relay. Each Act advances for exactly 32 seconds of foreground-active engine time, then moves through its own deterministic House praise. The engine and whole-table clock consume the same bounded frame delta, so a long main-thread stall cannot charge unseen Act time. Optional object density rises from five in the opening lane to nine in the home lane; the later Acts add original mandi, baraat, monsoon, and ghar scenery without introducing failure. The whole table also has a 240-second foreground-active boundary. Reaching that boundary before all five Acts closes the table without storing or displaying a completion. Pausing, hiding, or blurring suspends Acts and inter-Act transitions and resets the animation timestamp so background time cannot be charged on return.

Every action is optional. Leap uses input buffering, coyote time, and a generous held-height window; a second press in the air creates one air step. Dash becomes a contextual vault near low interference and an aerial stomp while airborne. Each Act supplies one fixed tool: Phone Pulse, Bargain Burst, Dhaaga Arc, Umbrella Wave, or the synchronized Ghar Flare. Their projectile grammars differ in reach, spread, arc, lift, and piercing behavior, but they can transform only allowlisted inanimate abstractions such as missed-call bubbles, price tags, puddles, produce baskets, route bubbles, streamers, and grocery lists. People, animals, vehicles, and buildings cannot enter a target set.

The route authors one broad temporary-effect gate per Act. Phulkari Guard transforms the next would-be collision, Chaa Overdrive strengthens the next tool action, and Monsoon Lift extends the next aerial sequence. These are temporary scene effects, not inventory or rewards: they have no counter, do not persist, reset at every Act, and may all be missed without changing content access, Act order, timing, closure, or completion. An unguarded collision creates one brief presentation-only stumble and witty line. It cannot change logical position, velocity, input availability, forward motion, or Act time, and one encountered interference cannot retrigger.

The presentation is a token-driven cinematic 2.5D action theatre rather than an imported arcade skin. Each Act has a deterministic Chandigarh skyline, four or more simulated depth layers, perspective road, Act color grade, layered Canvas lamps, and its own authored street layer: late-return lamps, mandi stalls, baraat ribbons, monsoon rain, or the home approach. Gurpreet and Harjit are smooth vector, role-specific figures with articulated run, leap, air-step, dash, vault, stomp, landing, collision, tool, and transformation responses. The logical stage remains `960 × 432`, device-pixel ratio remains capped at 2, camera response is capped at six logical pixels, effect particles remain capped at 24, active tool objects are capped at six, and synthesized audio voices remain capped at eight. No effect uses randomness, changes semantic progress, produces a full-canvas flash, or rapidly pulses luminance. Reduced motion uses the complete narrated route and suppresses parallax, camera response, flashes, after-images, dense rain/fog, and nonessential particles.

Resize redraws the current state without advancing the engine. Optional action layers, a sparse deterministic Act pulse, and curtain cues share one locally synthesized audio context; sound starts off, begins only after an explicit user choice, creates no queued resume cue, and is closed when sound is disabled or the route pauses, hides, blurs, exits, narrates, finishes, or reaches its boundary. Audio failure remains non-blocking and every cue has a visible/text equivalent.

The complete narrated route contains three rewritten action-set-piece beats for every Act and reaches the same curtain call, celebration, and `mode: "entertainment"` provenance. Reduced-motion visitors begin on that text route; narration never autoplays speech or sound. No timed response, precision movement, audio, animation, or visual interpretation is required to complete the table: Pause holds the authored boundary, remains paused between narrated Acts, and leaves the narrated beat controls available. Semantic Leap/Air step, Dash/Stomp, Act tool, Pause/Resume, and Narrated route controls back the Canvas action presentation.

Active session storage contains only `gameId`, authored Act position, local run identifier, and narrated story-beat position. Coordinates, elapsed time, input history, collisions, transformed objects, and performance summaries remain in memory and are not persisted. Because the remaining boundary budget is deliberately not stored, a Sector Sprint document reload fails closed to the Grand Salon, clears the active marker, explains the exit, and records no completion. Completion keeps the existing replaceable five-chapter entertainment fact in the Gallery. There is no assessment input or output.

All art is drawn from project-owned TypeScript and existing House tokens. No borrowed game name, character, level, image, sound, package, or runtime service is shipped. Nindova remains free and open source under Apache-2.0, with optional sound synthesized locally and zero telemetry or third-party runtime request. The engineering target is AAA-inspired audiovisual polish at premium indie browser scope. The repository can verify the richer original presentation, fixed behavior, responsiveness, and technical boundaries; it cannot verify an “AAA” quality label, cultural authenticity, or player enjoyment without representative adult playtesting.

This decision does not change Masala Mound or any Night Room rule. It does not claim that Sector Sprint makes a person sleepy, tired, culturally represented, or cognitively measured; those outcomes have no supporting human evidence in this repository.
