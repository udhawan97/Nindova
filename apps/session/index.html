01
It gets easier, not harder — effort decays until the game does almost nothing. Boredom doesn't sedate (d ≈ −0.03 vs. neutral; 2025 meta-analysis, N=6,570). The target is effortless absorption, not under-stimulation.
02
Naming a thing before you put it away is optional — the putting-away isn't. Writing specific unfinished tasks at bedtime sped sleep onset ~9 min in a polysomnography RCT (Scullin et al. 2018, d = 0.63). The more specific, the faster.
03
It ends on its own — a bounded session, then dark. Night-time screen harm is mostly unbounded time (~1 h displacement), not light (0–10 min). The product sells an ending.
04
It never mentions sleep — no scores, streaks, or tracking. Attention to one's own sleep performance is itself arousing (Spiegelhalder 2012, n=236, PSG; Espie's attention–intention–effort model).
05
A visible, completable set — closure, not interruption. Interrupted goals get resumed (Ovsiankina, 67%); bounded sets preserve the sense of control (CHI 2021: playlists 0% control loss vs. autoplay 87%).
06
Tonight's scene changes; the path never does. One arc, no menu. The procession of different animals is a stream of specific, unrelated imagery (imagery distraction beat no-instruction; vague distraction didn't — Harvey & Payne 2002). A game-select menu would rebuild the "one more" loop.
Nindova is a behavioral design study. The target outcome is earlier stopping and a shorter gap between getting into bed and trying to sleep — not "better sleep." This is not a treatment for insomnia; persistent sleep difficulty deserves CBT-I with a clinician. Designed simple enough for anyone, positioned 13+: evening light suppresses melatonin in young children even at the dimmest screen levels (Hartstein et al. 2022). The full iOS concept pairs this session with Screen Time interception set earlier in the day.
